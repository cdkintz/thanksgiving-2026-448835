Spatchcocked Traeger turkey

Cory

A 19 lb spatchcocked bird from Taylor's Market, dry brined for two days and smoked on the Traeger with a chicken fat herb paste. Adapted from Traeger's roasted spatchcock turkey.

Ingredients

  • One 19 lb turkey, spatchcocked
  • Dry brine: 2 1/2 tbsp fine pink salt (about 40 g), 2 tbsp cane sugar, 1 tbsp black pepper, 2 tsp baking powder; 2/3 under the skin
  • Herb paste: about 2/3 cup chicken fat, 1 head garlic, thyme and rosemary finely chopped, and about 1 tbsp dried oregano
  • Smoker tea: bay leaves, rosemary and thyme sprigs, orange peel and boiling water in a metal cup
  • Olive oil

Timeline

  • Tuesday: pick up, freeze the backbone for soup, dry brine and refrigerate uncovered; season the ground lamb and pork
  • Wednesday: leave it in the fridge so the skin dries
  • Thursday 12:15 pm: herb paste on
  • 12:50 pm: smoker tea in, preheat to 400°F
  • 1:15 pm: turkey on at 400°F
  • 1:45 pm: drop to 325°F
  • 2:30 pm on: check every 15 minutes; expect it done around 3:15 to 3:45
  • 4:45 pm: carve after resting. 5:00 pm: serve

Method

  1. Pull the breast at 157 to 160°F and the thighs at 170 to 175°F. Go by temperature, not the clock.
  2. Rest at least 30 minutes under foil. It holds well wrapped in a dry cooler if it finishes early.

Kosher challah stuffing with lamb, apricot and orange

Cory

Kosher and dairy-free. Based on The JC's lamb and apricot stuffing, with pumpkin seeds in place of chestnuts.

Ingredients

  • 2 large challah loaves (about 2 lb), cubed and dried
  • 1.5 lb ground lamb, seasoned with salt, garlic, fennel seed, cumin, cinnamon, black pepper and chili flakes
  • 1/4 cup chicken fat, plus more for the pans
  • 2 cups diced onion, 2 cups diced celery
  • 1 1/2 cups diced dried apricots
  • 2 oranges, zested and juiced
  • 1/4 cup sage, 2 tbsp each rosemary and thyme, 1/2 cup parsley
  • 1/2 cup toasted pumpkin seeds
  • 2 eggs (optional), 3 to 4 cups chicken stock, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Brown the seasoned lamb and set aside.
  2. Soften the onion and celery in chicken fat. Add the apricots, zest and herbs, then the orange juice.
  3. Toss with the challah, lamb, parsley and seeds. Add eggs and stock until moist.
  4. Bake covered at 350°F for 30 minutes, then uncovered for 20 to 25.

Traditional stuffing

Cory

Classic bread stuffing with pork sausage and butter. Contains dairy; not kosher.

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf rustic Italian bread (2 lb), cubed and dried
  • 1.5 lb plain ground pork, seasoned the night before with 1 1/2 tsp salt, 1 tbsp sage, 1 tsp thyme, 1 garlic clove, 1/2 tsp pepper, a pinch of allspice or cinnamon (optional), 1 tsp sugar and chili flakes
  • 2 large onions, 3 cups celery, 4 garlic cloves
  • 3 tbsp sage, 2 tbsp thyme, 1/2 cup parsley
  • 2 eggs, 4 to 5 cups chicken stock
  • 1 stick butter, plus more for the pans

Method

  1. Brown the sausage, then soften the onion and celery in its fat with the butter. Add garlic and herbs.
  2. Toss with the bread, sausage, parsley and eggs. Add stock slowly until moist but not soggy.
  3. Bake covered at 375°F for 30 minutes, then uncovered for 25.

Mashed Yukon Gold potatoes

Cory

Dairy-free. 10 lb fits one big pot and leaves some leftovers.

Ingredients

  • 10 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled
  • 1/2 cup chicken fat and 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1 head garlic, peeled
  • 3 thyme sprigs, 1 fresh bay leaf, a few peppercorns (optional: 1 small rosemary sprig)
  • 2 to 3 cups hot chicken stock
  • Kosher salt (2 tbsp per gallon of cooking water) and pepper

Method

  1. Cut into even 2 inch chunks. Start in cold salted water and simmer gently 15 to 20 minutes, until a knife slides in.
  2. Garlic confit (can be made Wednesday): melt the fat, add the oil, garlic, herbs and peppercorns, and keep at a lazy bubble for 15 to 20 minutes until the garlic is soft and pale gold. Remove the herbs and mash the garlic into the fat. If made ahead, refrigerate and use within 4 days. Heat the stock.
  3. Drain well, then steam dry in the empty pot over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes.
  4. Rice or hand mash. No blender or mixer.
  5. Fold in the garlic fat first, then the hot stock a cup at a time until creamy. Season.
  6. Hold warm in a slow cooker for up to 2 hours; loosen with hot stock before serving. Start around 3:30 pm.

Tips

  • Dry potatoes are fluffy potatoes. Fat before liquid keeps them creamy, not gluey.
  • Keep everything hot, and fold instead of beating. A splash of oat milk adds creaminess and stays dairy-free.

Roasted Brussels sprouts and green beans

Rachel

About 4 lb Brussels sprouts and 3 lb green beans. Olive oil, not butter, and no nut toppings.

  1. Halve the sprouts and trim the beans.
  2. Toss in olive oil, salt and pepper and spread on sheet pans.
  3. Roast at 425°F: 20 to 25 minutes for the sprouts, 12 to 15 for the beans.

Whipped brown butter with cranberry swirl

Cory

Contains dairy. Make the butter Wednesday; top it Thursday.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb butter, softened (brown 4 tbsp of it first)
  • 1/2 tsp brown miso, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tsp minced rosemary, zest of 1/2 orange, cracked pepper, pinch of salt
  • Optional: 2 to 3 tbsp whole milk
  • Topping: 1 cup cranberry sauce blended smooth with a pinch of salt; 8 to 10 sage leaves fried crisp; flaky salt

Method

  1. Brown 4 tbsp butter until nutty; chill until soft-solid. Mash the miso smooth with a spoonful of butter.
  2. Beat all the butter 6 to 8 minutes until pale and fluffy. Beat in the miso, honey, rosemary, zest, pepper, salt and milk.
  3. Refrigerate overnight. Thursday, soften 1 hour and re-whip 30 seconds.
  4. Spread in a shallow bowl, swirl the cooled cranberry on top, and finish with crispy sage, flaky salt and zest.
